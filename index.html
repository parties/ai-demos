AI demos for school and district staff

Each demo walks through one real task from school or district work. You see the prompt a staff member typed, the files they gave the AI, and what the AI wrote back, so you can judge whether it would help with your own work and what you would still need to check.

There are 31 demos, grouped by job. Every name and number in them is made up, and every page works without an internet connection.

For everyone: asking well and checking the answer

  • How to write a clear request: the four parts

    Start with a vague one-line request, then add what you want done, the format, the voice, and the background. Watch a board memo, a family letter, or a job posting get more usable with each part.

  • AI answers that look right but aren't: how to check them

    Three AI answers with mistakes planted in them: a policy summary, a budget total, and a legal question. Find the mistakes, then see the four checking habits that catch them.

  • Have the AI ask you questions before it writes

    The same request made two ways. Asked in one line, the AI returns a template full of blanks. Told to ask questions first, it returns a letter you could send.

Superintendent's office

  • Drafting a board memo from your notes

    Give the AI your rough notes and last month's memo. It drafts this month's memo in the same format and marks every figure you still need to confirm.

  • Summarizing a long board packet

    Give the AI a 148-page public board packet. It returns a one-page brief: the decisions due, the dollar amounts, and the items likely to draw questions, each with its page number.

  • Weighing the evidence before a decision

    Give the AI the documents behind a district decision. It lays out the case for and against with a source for each point, shows where the documents disagree, and lists the questions still to ask.

  • Practicing for a difficult meeting

    The AI plays a skeptical board member, an upset parent, or a reporter. You answer its questions, and it gives you feedback on each answer.

Human capital and HR

  • Writing a job posting from a duties list

    Give the AI the duties list you already have. It writes a posting in district format and flags jargon and requirements that would turn away qualified applicants, with the reason for each.

  • Building an interview kit that treats every candidate the same

    Give the AI one job posting. It writes structured interview questions and a scoring guide, so every candidate is asked and scored the same way. People do the screening; the AI never ranks candidates.

  • Answering staff questions from the benefits packet

    Give the AI the benefits and onboarding packet. It answers the 20 questions HR hears every August, and each answer points to the packet section it came from.

  • Reading contract language closely

    Give the AI a contract clause to get a plain-English explanation. Or give it the handbook and the negotiated agreement to find every place they contradict each other.

Information services

  • Reviewing a vendor's privacy policy

    Give the AI a vendor's privacy policy and the district's privacy checklist. It checks each item and quotes the line it based its answer on. The approval decision stays with you.

  • Turning fix notes into a staff how-to

    Give the AI a technician's short notes on a fix. It writes numbered steps any staff member can follow, so the same ticket stops coming back.

  • Writing a staff notice about planned maintenance

    Give the AI a technical maintenance plan. It writes a calm notice for staff: what will change, when, and what they need to do.

  • Getting help with spreadsheet formulas

    Describe the result you need and get the formula. Or paste a formula or script you inherited and get a line-by-line explanation, including what could go wrong.

Curriculum and instructional technology

  • Checking a course against the standards

    Give the AI a course outline and a standards list. It returns a coverage table showing which standards are missing, repeated, or only partly covered.

  • Comparing curriculum samples against a rubric

    Give the AI three publishers' samples and the adoption rubric. It scores each sample and quotes the sentence behind every score, so the committee can check it.

  • Following up after a PD session

    Give the AI the facilitator's rough notes from one session. It writes a handout, an exit question, and the follow-up email to participants.

  • Rewriting course catalog descriptions

    Give the AI course descriptions written by many teachers over many years. It rewrites them in one format families can read, and flags missing facts instead of inventing them.

Wellness

  • Finding patterns in student data without exposing students

    Start with an attendance, behavior, and wellness export. Remove names and ID numbers first, on screen, then look at the patterns by time and place.

  • Sorting survey comments into themes

    Give the AI hundreds of anonymous survey comments. It groups them into themes with counts and real quotes, and flags any comment that needs a person's attention today.

  • Building a community resource guide for families

    Give the AI a messy list of community agencies. It writes a family guide sorted by need, and flags missing hours and phone numbers before it goes to print.

Teaching, learning and culture: secondary

  • Planning a data meeting

    Give the AI a de-identified data summary. It writes a timed agenda and discussion questions for a principals' data meeting.

  • Passing a district directive on to building staff

    Give the AI a dense central-office directive. It writes a one-page brief for the principal and five minutes of talking points for the staff meeting.

  • Creating shared intervention forms

    Give the AI notes on how four buildings each record interventions today. It compares their practices side by side and drafts blank Tier 1, 2, and 3 forms every building can use.

Communications

  • Adapting one message for every family

    One district notice rewritten at three reading levels, in two tones, in English and Spanish, side by side.

  • Combining department submissions into one newsletter

    Give the AI eight submissions written eight different ways. It combines them into one newsletter in one voice and lists the conflicts to check before it goes out.

  • Writing a holding statement when the facts are still coming in

    Give the AI only the facts you have confirmed. It writes a short holding statement and lists the questions reporters will likely ask next, marking which ones you can answer yet.

  • Adapting one announcement for each channel

    Give the AI one memo. It writes a website post, a social media post, and a text alert, each checked against that channel's length limit.

Programs and operations

  • Drafting an event schedule and finding conflicts

    Give the AI the rooms, presenters, and scheduling limits. It drafts a schedule grid and lists the conflicts it could not solve, for you to decide.

  • Writing a grant report from program data

    Give the AI a table of program numbers. It drafts the needs, outcomes, and next-steps sections of a grant report. Click any sentence to see the numbers behind it.
