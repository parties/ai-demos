Finding patterns in student data without exposing students

Attendance, behavior, and wellness records can show a district where students need help, and an AI can find patterns in thousands of rows quickly. But student records must never be pasted into an AI tool as they come out of the student information system. This page shows the safe order. A district data staff member exports the records, then removes names, ID numbers, birthdates, and staff notes on the district's own system. Only the de-identified rows go to the AI, and every pattern it reports is about a day, a class period, a grade, or a building, never a single student.

Work through the three steps in order. In step 2, press the button to watch each rule applied and to see the check that nothing identifying is left. All records on this page are fabricated; no real students appear.

1The raw export

This is what an attendance, behavior, and wellness export looks like when it comes out of the student information system. Each record carries the student's name, ID number, and birthdate, and some carry a staff note. In this form it must not be pasted into any AI tool.

Raw export from the student information system

2Remove what identifies students (done by the district)

A district staff member does this step on screen, on the district's own system, before anything goes to an AI. Press the button to apply each rule in turn. The page then scans every row to confirm that no name, ID number, birthdate, or staff note is left. Only these de-identified rows move on to step 3.

  1. Remove names
  2. Replace student ID with a random alias; the key stays with the data owner
  3. Remove birthdates; grade is enough
  4. Remove staff notes; free text hides names
  5. Suppress small groups: any count under 5 shows as "<5"

3The patterns the AI found AI-generated

The AI receives only the de-identified rows from step 2. It counts absences, behavior incidents, and wellness check-ins by day, class period, grade, and building, and describes what stands out.

What this can't tell you

  • Why. A period spike could be lunch, one schedule, or one hallway. The data says where to look; people find out why.
  • Who. Every result is about a time, a place, or a grade, never a student.
  • These records are fabricated and the patterns were built in to show the method. Real data will be messier.

Before you use it

These patterns tell you where to look, not why something is happening or which students are involved. Someone who knows the buildings has to find the reason, for example by talking with the staff who teach period 5. Before you share any result, check that no group is small enough to point to a student; any count under 5 should show as "<5".

All records are fabricated; no real students appear on this page. Every name, number, and record is made up. The patterns in step 3 are worked out by the page itself, standing in for the AI, so the page works without an internet connection.
